Exponential moving averages (EMA) of neural-network weights can improve a
model during training, but their relative benefit depends on the trajectory
being averaged. We present a new manuscript revision of a completed, bounded
two-dimensional diffusion study that crossed terminal duration with the full
optimizer learning-rate policy. The retained experiment compared raw weights
with EMA decays of 0.99 and 0.999 on two synthetic datasets, using

These results describe the selected model, datasets and budgets. They agree
qualitatively with prior work connecting averaging and annealing, but do not
identify an endpoint-only mechanism, an optimal decay, or a general EMA benefit.
The revision adds a critical literature map and portable paper sources without
rerunning or altering the historical experiment.
